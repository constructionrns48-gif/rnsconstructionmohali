New Kothi Design Ideas for Mohali and Chandigarh in 2026

If you are planning to build a new kothi in Mohali or Chandigarh, one of the first questions is what it should look like. Tricity architects and design platforms are featuring a clear change from the heavy columns, domes and mirror glass of the 2000s towards cleaner, more climate-aware homes. This guide rounds up the kothi design directions featured in their recent work and 2026 guides, and what to keep in mind before choosing one. It reflects published designs as of October 2026 - a snapshot of featured ideas, not a measured ranking of popularity.

1. Clean modern elevations

Tricity architects are featuring a move away from ornate columns and reflective glass towards fluted stone, warm wood finishes, large but shaded glazing and flat or low parapet roofs. The kothis they showcase for Mohali's developing sectors aim to look well-proportioned rather than loud - a disciplined material palette, ordered openings and a real entrance instead of a decorated one.

2. The statement entrance

A tall, framed entrance appears repeatedly in current kothi design guides: a double-height entrance lobby behind glass, flanked by stone-clad piers, or a deep portico with a full-width first-floor balcony above it. Tricity design studios also showcase grand entrance foyers and statement lighting in their luxury home work. The idea is presence without clutter.

3. Mixed-material facades

Design guides for 2026 favour combinations over single-material fronts: stone cladding with wood-look panels, exposed brick bands, and metal or concrete accents. High-Pressure Laminate (HPL) panels and vertical WPC louvers can replicate the look of teak or oak, but how well they stand up to monsoon rain and summer sun depends on the exterior grade, specification and installation - worth confirming with your builder. A balanced mix of two or three materials, not five, keeps an elevation calm.

4. Chandigarh's modernist influence

Chandigarh gave India its own modernist design vocabulary - flat roofs, raw concrete and the brise-soleil sun-breaker - and elevation designs featured for the region borrow from it: deep louvers and screens that shade glass from the Punjab summer while giving the facade a calm, geometric character. It is a local style with a practical purpose.

5. Traditional-modern fusion

For families who do not want a fully contemporary look, fusion designs keep familiar elements - carved jharokha-style balconies, arches or a jaali screen - and combine them with glass railings and minimal plaster. The heritage character stays; the finish is current.

6. Climate-smart planning for Tricity weather

Tricity summers are hot and winters genuinely cold, and architects stress orientation: living spaces placed for even north and east daylight, a shorter and shaded west face, and measured, deeply shaded glazing to the south. For northern India, 2026 design guides emphasise double-glazed windows, insulated facades and projecting balconies that provide summer shading. Orientation is worth getting right at the planning stage, because it shapes comfort and cooling costs for the life of the house.

7. Indoor-outdoor living

Tricity house-plan guides for 2026 feature indoor-outdoor living prominently: landscaped lawns, usable terraces, courtyards and balconies that connect to the main living areas. Large sliding openings and shaded sit-outs can extend daily life outside without exposing interiors to the afternoon sun.

8. Greener and smarter homes

Current design guides give more space to sustainable and smart features: vertical greenery walls, solar-ready roofs and shading screens that reduce heat gain. Smart-home features - automated lighting, security and climate control - are often included from the design stage, and facade lighting and EV charging points can be planned into the structure rather than added later.

9. Before you finalise a design

A few practical points matter more than any trend:

  • Plot rules first: the sector's zoning plan governs ground coverage, setbacks, height and basement rights before a single room is drawn. The applicable rules are plot-specific, so confirm them for your plot before finalising a design.
  • One Tricity design studio argues that reused, standardised builder plans are a primary reason so many local kothis look alike; a custom design is what creates a distinct facade.
  • Orientation and ventilation shape everyday comfort as much as any finish.
  • Trends age; proportion, light and build quality do not. Choose a design your family will still like in fifteen years.

Mohali & Chandigarh Construction Updates 2026

Updated: 27 October 2026

Location: Mohali, SAS Nagar, New Chandigarh and Chandigarh

Introduction

Construction is not just about roads, bridges and buildings. Well-planned infrastructure can make everyday life easier by improving connectivity, reducing waterlogging, supporting local businesses and strengthening essential public services.

This roundup of construction updates from Mohali, Chandigarh and the wider Tricity region covers government development plans and civil works: road improvements, drainage, water supply, public spaces and urban infrastructure. Understanding these projects helps residents, homebuyers, business owners and construction professionals make better-informed decisions - whether you are following GMADA projects, buying property in New Chandigarh, or planning home construction in Mohali.

This article examines documented projects and official records, explains their potential public benefits, and distinguishes planned work from confirmed completion.

1. Mohali's Next Generation Roads Programme

One of the most significant infrastructure development initiatives in Mohali is the Next Generation Roads Programme. According to The Tribune's reporting (25 March 2026), it covers 83.4 km of major roads and junctions in three phases:

  • Phase 1: 41.4 km
  • Phase 2: 21.6 km
  • Phase 3: 20.4 km

The project cost was revised from Rs 783.46 crore to Rs 666.41 crore through an official corrigendum after a change in scope. Under the hybrid annuity model, the contractor is also responsible for maintaining these roads for 10 years.

Where it stands: In April 2026, The Tribune reported (18 April 2026) that a letter of award had been issued to Gawar Construction Limited, whose Rs 673.99 crore bid was the lowest evaluated offer, with a reported total outgo of Rs 912.63 crore including annuity over the 10-year concession. At that time the award remained subject to two writ petitions before the Punjab and Haryana High Court and a possible further reduction in scope. Road works were continuing in Mohali as of October 2026 - The Tribune reported on 4 October 2026 that construction was in progress on one side of the alternative airport road, according to GMADA officials.

The award details above were reported in April 2026. Court proceedings and official notices may have changed the position since then, so the programme should not be assumed to be fully under construction or completed.

How can residents benefit?

  • Better road surfaces may improve daily commuting.
  • Improved junctions can help traffic move more smoothly.
  • A 10-year maintenance commitment can reduce damage caused by deteriorating surfaces.
  • Better connectivity may help shops, offices and other local businesses reach customers.

Actual travel-time savings and safety improvements will depend on the work completed at individual locations.

2. New Chandigarh: Road Connectivity and Urban Development

Road connectivity is an important part of New Chandigarh's development, and the Greater Mohali Area Development Authority (GMADA) has published details of several projects on its official infrastructure page.

Its published information describes an approximately 8 km, 200-foot-wide road connecting the New Chandigarh/UT boundary to the Kurali-Siswan road T-junction, at a cost of Rs 230 crore including land. GMADA has separately described proposals for additional connecting roads linked to the PR-4 corridor. Land acquisition and implementation status must be checked separately for each road on GMADA's official notices.

Potential benefits for the public

  • Improved connections between residential areas and adjoining towns.
  • More convenient access to employment, education and healthcare.
  • Better movement of goods and services.
  • Improved access for residents and businesses as supporting infrastructure develops.

However, new roads alone do not guarantee lower property prices, higher investment returns or shorter commutes. These outcomes depend on traffic management, public transport, actual project completion and the location of homes and workplaces.

3. Water Supply: Essential Infrastructure for Growing Communities

Reliable water supply is fundamental to residential and commercial development.

GMADA's published infrastructure plans discuss a dedicated water-treatment facility for New Chandigarh, including a proposed 20 MGD water-treatment plant associated with planned canal-water supply. New water-supply works are procured through the authority's official tender notices, where residents and contractors can follow current procurements.

A published plan or tender notice indicates intent or procurement activity; it does not prove that a work is complete or that additional water is already reaching households.

How can the public benefit?

  • Additional water infrastructure can support growing residential communities.
  • Properly maintained supply systems can help improve service reliability.
  • Better planning can support homes, schools, shops and other public facilities.
  • Monitoring water availability can help residents understand local development needs.

The actual benefit depends on groundwater availability, water quality, system capacity and the commissioning of the completed works.

4. Chandigarh: Storm-Water Drainage and Waterlogging Prevention

Drainage infrastructure is particularly important during heavy rainfall, and Chandigarh's Municipal Corporation procures drainage construction and maintenance works through the Chandigarh UT e-procurement portal, where current tenders and their status are published officially.

Why does drainage construction matter?

  • Well-designed drainage can help rainwater flow away from roads and built-up areas.
  • Regular cleaning can reduce blockages caused by silt and waste.
  • Better drainage can help protect roads, building entrances and surrounding infrastructure.
  • Effective storm-water management can improve public convenience during rainy weather.

No drainage project can eliminate all flooding. Results depend on rainfall intensity, drain capacity, maintenance and the condition of the wider drainage network.

5. Why Construction Quality Matters for Every Resident

Infrastructure development creates value only when projects are appropriately designed, built and maintained. Anyone planning a new house or renovation in Mohali should pay attention to:

  • Structural safety: ask for suitable structural design and engineering supervision for the building and its foundation.
  • Soil investigation: site-specific soil testing helps engineers assess ground conditions and determine appropriate foundation design.
  • Waterproofing and drainage: proper roof slopes, terrace waterproofing and rainwater drainage help reduce seepage and water damage.
  • Material quality: construction materials should meet applicable specifications and be checked during execution.
  • Transparent estimates: a written scope of work, material specifications, payment milestones and completion terms can help prevent misunderstandings.
  • Approvals: verify the applicable building-plan approval, land-use permissions and other requirements with the relevant authority before starting construction.

These practices can help property owners make more informed decisions, although they cannot guarantee that every future maintenance issue will be avoided.

6. What These Developments Mean for Homebuyers and Local Businesses

Infrastructure development can influence how people travel, access services and use residential or commercial property.

For homebuyers, it is sensible to evaluate road access, water supply, drainage, nearby facilities and approved land use before buying a plot or house.

For local businesses, improved connectivity may make premises easier to reach and support the movement of customers and suppliers.

For construction businesses, official portals such as the GMADA tender notices and the Chandigarh UT e-procurement portal provide information about procurement opportunities in civil works, drainage, water supply, road maintenance and public facilities.

Important: A proposed project or published tender is not proof of a completed project, a guaranteed increase in property value or a confirmed business opportunity.

Conclusion

Infrastructure development in Mohali and Chandigarh deserves attention because roads, water supply, drainage and public facilities affect everyday life.

The most useful way to follow construction news is to look beyond project announcements. Check the official notice, its current status, the scope of work, implementation progress and the public service it is intended to improve.

Better-informed residents can make better property decisions, while construction professionals - from individual contractors to every construction company in Mohali - can identify relevant opportunities without relying on unverified claims.

Latest · Building Rules · Sep 2026

Punjab building rules amended - in force from 7 September 2026

The Punjab Urban Planning and Development Building (Second Amendment) Rules, 2026 were notified on 2 September and published in the Punjab Government Gazette (Extraordinary) on 7 September 2026, in force from that date. Key changes in the official notification (G.S.R.52): maximum floor-to-floor height for booths, shops and shop-cum-offices raised to 5.0 metres - this does not apply to shops or offices inside shopping malls, standalone commercial buildings, multiplexes or mixed-use projects; rooftop solar panel structures up to 2.4 metres clear height no longer count towards building height or FAR; a toilet up to 6 sq m is now permitted on the terrace; a new clearance zone applies for buildings near high-tension lines. These rules apply in areas governed by the Punjab Urban Planning and Development Building Rules, 2021, which cover the GMADA planning area. Note: these provisions are not a blanket permission for every property - always verify the applicable local authority rules and approvals for your specific plot or building before planning. Sources: Punjab Government Gazette, 7 Sep 2026 (official record copy) and Hindustan Times, 10 Sep 2026. Verified 13 Oct 2026.

Eco City 3, New Chandigarh gets environmental clearance

GMADA's planned Eco City 3 township in New Chandigarh (Mullanpur) has received environmental clearance from Punjab's State Environment Impact Assessment Authority. The township covers 716 acres across nine villages, with residential plots to be allotted by draw of lots and commercial sites by open auction. Important: clearance is an approval milestone, not a launch - no public plot booking, draw dates or prices were found on the GMADA pages checked on 13 October 2026 (GMADA's Eco City 3 notification page and gmada.gov.in). Guide · Tricity

Civil vs turnkey construction - which should you choose?

Civil construction covers brick work and plaster - material and labour included. It suits owners who want to manage finishing separately or complete the home in stages. Turnkey construction covers design, construction and interiors through to key handover - one team and one clear quotation. Guide · Floor Additions

Planning to add a floor? Check the structure first

A first-floor or second-floor addition starts with the existing building, not the new one. The foundation and columns must be assessed for the extra load before scope or cost is agreed. Send existing plans and photos to begin the discussion, and expect an on-site structural review before a final quotation. Planned right, an addition also lets you refresh the elevation at the same time.
