Owner planning guide

RCC Slab Checklist: Questions to Review With Your Engineer Before Pouring

The useful time to ask about a slab is before the pour, while the drawings, reinforcement and service routes can still be reviewed. This RCC slab checklist helps an owner organise that conversation. It is not a design, a site approval or permission to proceed.

19 November 2026 · RNS Construction Mohali

Ask who gives the go-ahead

Know which engineer is responsible for inspecting the work and who records the decision to proceed. Ask for the latest structural drawings to be available on site, with any changes clearly resolved. A contractor's delivery booking should not be mistaken for engineering clearance.

An owner can ask questions and keep records, but cannot verify structural adequacy from a checklist or a photograph. Bar sizes, spacing, slab thickness, laps, cover, supports and openings must follow the project design and the responsible engineer's instructions.

Review the supports and reinforcement together

Ask the site team whether the formwork, supports and reinforcement have been inspected against the drawings. Find out whether any issue remains open. Do not move props, bend bars or adjust the assembly yourself to make it look like an online example.

Keep an agreed record of the pre-pour inspection. Photographs can help explain what was visible at a particular time, but they do not replace measurements, test records or the engineer's assessment. If the built arrangement differs from the drawing, ask who has reviewed the difference.

Resolve the routes that will become hidden

Walk through the intended positions of openings, sleeves, conduits and junctions with the relevant teams. Confirm that they are coordinated with the structural design and architectural layout. A late request for a drain or an electrical point needs review, not a last-minute hole or a casual instruction to shift reinforcement.

This is also a chance to ask how future maintenance access will work. Mark queries before the pour and keep the agreed revision or instruction with the job records. Do not rely on a message sent to one worker as the only record of a design change.

Agree testing, placement and curing responsibilities

Ask what concrete specification applies, how delivery and placement will be managed, which checks or samples are required, and who keeps the results. These are project-specific matters for the responsible team. Avoid adding water or changing a mix on an owner's informal instruction.

Ask for the curing method, water arrangements, start conditions and duration to be planned before work begins. The correct approach depends on the concrete, cement, exposure, weather and specification. A generic number of days from a blog should not override the engineer's instructions.

Keep a clear stop-and-ask route

The owner should know whom to call if something changes, such as a drawing conflict, a disturbed support, a service-route clash or an unplanned interruption. Stay out of active work zones and do not touch temporary works. Raise concerns with the responsible professional rather than trying to inspect a live pour at close quarters.

For a project enquiry with RNS, share the location, current drawings and stage of work. A written site-specific scope is more useful than comparing a slab only by area or a headline rate. Engineering inspection and any clearance remain separate from reading this guide.

Make the review specific to your drawings

Ask the responsible engineer to adapt an RCC slab inspection checklist to the current drawing revision and the work on site. A slab casting checklist should name who checks each item and who records the decision to proceed. For a roof, an RCC roof slab checklist also needs to reflect the actual openings, drainage interfaces and service routes, rather than copying another building's arrangement.

Settle preparation questions before delivery

Review the checklist before slab casting with the site team early enough to deal with open questions. A concrete slab preparation checklist can record access, delivery arrangements and the agreed inspection sequence. Ask who is responsible for reinforcement checking before slab casting, what evidence will be retained and how a difference from the drawings will be referred back to the engineer.

Keep temporary works and structural checks distinct

Ask which competent person reviews the slab shuttering checklist and the stability of the temporary works. A beam reinforcement checklist must come from the project design and professional inspection, not an owner's visual guess. Keep the concrete pouring checklist connected to those reviews so that arranging a pump or a delivery is not mistaken for permission to start.

Record what the team will do if conditions change

An RCC slab quality inspection should have a clear record of the findings and any outstanding action. Discuss slab casting precautions with the responsible team, including how they handle an interruption or an unexpected site condition. Agree who manages concrete curing after slab casting and where the project-specific method, timing and checks will be recorded.

Ask for the basis of technical decisions

Have the engineer explain the roof slab construction process in terms of your drawings and the planned work sequence. Questions about RCC slab thickness standards need the applicable design basis and professional judgement, not a universal dimension from a blog. Keep slab construction quality control tied to that design, the specified materials, inspection records and the person authorised to accept the work.

For the actual site

This guide helps you ask informed questions. It does not replace project drawings, the contract, a qualified inspection or advice on the rules that apply to your property.
